## The Big Picture

- **2024 budget:** 4.35% tax rate increase on a residential property assessed at $300K = $40.47/year
- **Net levy increase:** 5.96% (below the 6.1% guideline)
- **33% of increase:** existing base services (salaries, inflation)
- **34% of increase:** capital reserve contributions (infrastructure)
- **25% of increase:** Service Level Changes (SLCs) — new positions for modernization, strategic plan
- **8% of increase:** hospital contributions

**The key phrase in the budget:** "positions to support the **modernization of service delivery and operations**" — this is literally their stated priority. They're asking for it.

## The Continuous Improvement Unit (CIU) — Your Entry Point

This is the most interesting thing in the entire budget. Muskoka has a **Continuous Improvement Unit** whose mandate is:

1. "Implement or support activities to advance the Action Plans for the District Strategic Plan 2023-2026"
2. "Continue to identify and implement opportunities for **innovation and continuous improvement**"
3. "Project lead the **transition of corporate services like finance, human resources and information technology** from The Pines and Fairvern Long-Term Care Homes to District systems"
4. "**Partner with Area Municipalities to identify opportunities to increase operational savings, secure alternative funding sources, and improve municipal efficiency through shared services or modernization**"

This is literally a department whose job is to find efficiency improvements and modernize service delivery. They're not just a target — they're the **internal champion** you need. If the CIU director sees a demo of AI-assisted permit triage or council minutes processing, THEY bring it to council. You don't have to.

## What This Means for the Municipal AI Plan

### The opening

Muskoka has:
1. A stated priority to "modernize service delivery and operations" (in the budget)
2. A department dedicated to "innovation and continuous improvement" (the CIU)
3. A Strategic Plan 2023-2026 that drives spending (need to read)
4. Legacy technology (iCreate CMS, PDF-based planning, manual meeting minutes)
5. Seasonal capacity strain (60K permanent, 81K seasonal, 2.1M visitors)
6. A two-tier structure with 6 lower-tier municipalities — fragmentation = opportunity for shared services

### The pitch shape

Not "buy an AI platform." Instead:

> "We can help your Continuous Improvement Unit deliver on the Strategic Plan's modernization goals. Here's a demonstration of AI-assisted council minutes processing. It takes a 2-hour meeting recording and produces structured action items in 3 minutes. We can do the same for permit triage, FOI request processing, and citizen inquiry routing. It runs on your hardware — no citizen data leaves your network. We implement, train your staff, and you own it."

### Who to approach

1. **The CIU director** — they're the internal champion. Their job is to find efficiency.
2. **Commissioner of Finance and Corporate Services** (Suzanne Olimer, per the budget) — she wrote the budget report and oversees IT.
3. **The CAO** — overall administrative leader.

Not council first. Staff first. Get them interested, then they bring it to council.

### What to build first (the demo)

Based on what's in the budget:

1. **Council minutes → actions pipeline** (you've already built this pattern for RTR)
2. **Document search across departments** (local RAG with their public documents)
3. **Budget variance dashboard** (same pattern as your governance dashboard)

All three are things you've already built. Different domain, same architecture.
